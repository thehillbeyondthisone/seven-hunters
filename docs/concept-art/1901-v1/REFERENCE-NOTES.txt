SEVEN HUNTERS — CONCEPT REFERENCE SET v1
Eilean Mor, Flannan Isles | Game date: 3 January 1901
Prepared 2 October 2026 using built-in image_gen.

PURPOSE
Researched visual concepts for atmosphere, architectural type, materials and period objects. These are not measured reconstructions, surveyed terrain, production model sheets or screenshots of the game.

CORRECTIONS TO EXISTING PROJECT ASSUMPTIONS
The original 1899 optic was hyper-radial, not the smaller third-order lens in modern summaries. This resolves the modern-optic uncertainty in docs/PLAN.md. Use the museum photograph for modelling.
HES describes a serpentine cast-iron staircase with a timber handrail. The demo's stone-tread description is not historical confirmation.
The 1901 station was recently built and maintained. Do not make it a century-old ruin.
Modern solar panels, gantry, automation equipment and replacement railings are not period references.
Exact 1901 paint colours remain unverified; restrained ochre trim is inferred from the later station.
Weather, illumination, viewpoints and fictional keeper are art direction, not recorded conditions or a historical portrait.

PLATES
1. 01-station-approach.png — The station approach
Evidence: The real tower silhouette, flat-roofed house, diagonal lantern glazing and masonry enclosure guide this plate.
Reconstruction: Reconstructed rail alignment, facade details, trim colour and camera position. Do not trace this as a measured site plan.

2. 02-east-landing.png — Arrival at the east landing
Evidence: The contemporary record establishes an east landing and supply access; period concrete steps, ironwork and lifting gear shape the scene.
Reconstruction: The cliff profile, crane design and position, stair route and tower sightline are compositional reconstructions.

3. 03-keepers-kitchen.png — The keeper’s kitchen
Evidence: The reports describe a kitchen fireplace, cleaned utensils, clocks, bedrooms, work slate and log. The room should feel orderly and lived in.
Reconstruction: Room layout, range model, floor, furniture, lamp model and object placement are period-plausible choices, not recovered inventory.

4. 04-original-optic.png — Keeping the original light
Evidence: The large 1899 Henry-Lepaute hyper-radial bivalve optic and paired bullseyes follow the museum photograph.
Reconstruction: Burner detail, clockwork case, room clearances, hatch and light effects remain reconstructed. Use the original photograph for lens modelling.

VISUAL QA AND LIMITS
All four selected PNGs were viewed. The final selection excludes modern solar equipment, glass-fronted stove styling, invented horizon islands, tropical vegetation and close waves at lantern-room height.
The archival paired-bullseye arrangement and the immense original lens drive the optic concept. Burner detail, clockwork and optical light effects are interpretive; do not treat pictured mechanisms as engineering evidence.
The station image does not establish exact window counts, railway gauge/alignment, chapel footprint or historic railing patterns.
The landing image does not establish east-landing geometry, stair counts, crane type, tower sightline or cliff measurements. It is a materials and route proposal.
The kitchen is a period-plausible furnishing study. Its dimensions, floor, furniture, stove and lantern models and object positions are not recovered inventory.
No overturned chair, untouched meal or invented journal quote is used as evidence.
Concept images have not been promoted into the game. No performance, runtime, device or user-acceptance claim is made by this art task.
